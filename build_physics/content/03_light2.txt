H2 Refraction of light
P **Refraction** is the bending of light when it passes obliquely from one medium to another (the speed changes, so the direction changes).
IMGS L03_p2_0 | Refraction of light — incident, refracted rays and the normal (source card in Hindi/English);;L03_p2_1 | Refraction of a wave front: the wavelength shortens in the slower medium while the frequency stays the same
H3 Laws of refraction (Snell's law)
B (i) The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.
B (ii) The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media:
EQ sin i / sin r = μ_{2}/μ_{1} = _{1}μ_{2}   (refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1)
H3 Refractive index
EQ μ = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in medium = c/v
B Light bends **towards the normal** when it enters a denser medium (slows down) and **away from the normal** when it enters a rarer medium (speeds up).
B Refractive index has no unit; μ (vacuum) = 1, air ≈ 1.0003, water = 1.33 (4/3), glass ≈ 1.5 (3/2), diamond = 2.42.
PYQ 66th Q6 "Which does not change when light travels from one medium to another?" → **frequency** (velocity, wavelength change; refractive index is a property of the medium).
ADD Useful relations (not spelled out in the lectures) :: λ_{medium} = λ_{vacuum}/μ and v = c/μ, while ν is fixed by the source. Real depth / apparent depth = μ (a pond of depth 4 m looks 3 m deep through water). Normal incidence (i = 0) → no bending, only a change of speed. Optical density ≠ mass density (kerosene is less dense than water but optically denser).
H3 Everyday examples of refraction
B **1. Bending of an object** — a pencil in a glass of water appears bent at the surface (light from the immersed part bends away from the normal on leaving water).
B **2. Fish appears raised in water** — the apparent position of the fish is above its actual position, so a spear-fisher aims below the image.
B **3. Early sunrise and late sunset** — atmospheric refraction bends sunlight around the curvature of the Earth; the Sun is seen about **2 minutes before** actual sunrise and **2 minutes after** actual sunset, and appears flattened (oval) near the horizon.
B **4. Twinkling of stars** — the refractive index of atmospheric layers fluctuates (hot and cold air), so the refracted starlight keeps changing its path and intensity. Planets, being extended sources, do not twinkle appreciably.

H2 Total internal reflection (TIR)
B When light travels from a **denser to a rarer** medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the **critical angle (C)**, the light is completely reflected back into the denser medium instead of being refracted.
B Two conditions: (1) light must go from denser to rarer medium; (2) angle of incidence > critical angle.
EQ sin C = 1/μ   (critical angle: the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction is 90°)
IMGS L03_p3_5 | Refraction, critical angle and total internal reflection at a water–air surface;;L03_p4_0 | Reflection vs refraction when a ray meets a faster (rarer) medium
H3 Examples of total internal reflection
B **I. Sparkling of diamond** — refractive index of diamond is 2.42 (source: 2.5), so its critical angle is only **≈ 24°**. Diamond is cut so that light entering it meets the faces at i > C again and again, undergoes repeated TIR and finally beams out from a few faces into the observer's eye — hence the sparkle.
B **II. Optical fibre** — light propagates through a thin glass/quartz pipe (core, μ_{1}) surrounded by cladding of lower refractive index (μ_{2} < μ_{1}) by multiple total internal reflections along the axis, even when the fibre is bent. Used in telecommunication and endoscopy.
B **III. Mirage and looming** — in deserts/hot regions the refractive index of air near the hot surface becomes smaller than that above; light from a distant object bends successively away from the normal until i > C and TIR takes place, so an **inverted image** appears on the ground like a pool of water (mirage). **Looming** is the reverse in cold regions (warm air above cold air) — objects like ships appear raised in the sky.

CORR Source prints μ(diamond) = 2.5; the accepted value is **2.42** (C ≈ 24.4°). Glass μ = 1.5 gives C ≈ 42°; water μ = 1.33 gives C ≈ 49°.
ADD Other TIR examples worth remembering :: Totally reflecting prisms (45°–90°–45°) in binoculars and periscopes (Unit 4); brilliance of air bubbles in water; a test tube held obliquely in water looks silvery; "cat's-eye" road reflectors; endoscope (medical) and fibre-optic broadband. A rainbow involves refraction + *internal* (not total) reflection inside drops.

H2 Lenses
P A **lens** is a transparent optical medium bounded by two surfaces, at least one of which is curved. It works on the principle of **refraction** of light.

H3 Lens formula, magnification and power
EQ 1/f = 1/v – 1/u      (f = focal length, v = image distance, u = object distance)
EQ m = h_{i}/h_{o} = v/u      (h_{i} = height of image, h_{o} = height of object)
EQ Power of a lens  P = 1 / f (in metres)      unit: **dioptre (D)**
B Convex lens: **positive** power (f positive); concave lens: **negative** power (f negative). A lens of f = 50 cm has P = +2 D; f = –25 cm gives P = –4 D.

ADD Combination and quick facts (not in source) :: Powers of thin lenses in contact simply add: P = P_{1} + P_{2}. A convex lens immersed in a liquid of higher refractive index becomes diverging (air bubble in water acts as a concave lens). Focal length of a lens depends on colour — least for violet, most for red (chromatic aberration). Sunlight focused by a convex lens can burn paper because the image of the Sun forms at F.

H2 The human eye
P The human eye is a natural optical instrument that works like a camera — it forms images using refraction of light and allows us to see objects.
IMGS L04_p3_3 | Anatomy of the human eye — cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina, optic nerve;;L04_p3_4 | Sectional view — aqueous humour, vitreous humour, ciliary muscle, fovea, blind spot
H3 Main components of the eye
B **1. Cornea** — transparent, outermost layer; first surface where light enters; provides **maximum refraction** (bending) of light; acts like a fixed convex lens.
B **2. Aqueous humour** — clear fluid between cornea and lens; maintains intraocular pressure; nourishes cornea and lens.
B **3. Iris** — coloured part of the eye; controls the size of the pupil; works like the diaphragm of a camera.
B **4. Pupil** — central opening in the iris; regulates the amount of light entering the eye; expands in dim light, contracts in bright light.
B **5. Eye lens** — transparent, flexible **convex** lens; focuses light on the retina; can change its shape (**accommodation**): thick → near objects, thin → distant objects.
B **6. Ciliary muscles** — surround the lens; control its curvature; responsible for the power of accommodation.
B **7. Vitreous humour** — jelly-like substance behind the lens; maintains the shape of the eyeball; helps in light transmission.
B **8. Retina** — light-sensitive inner layer containing photoreceptors: **rods** → vision in dim light, **cones** → colour vision. The image formed here is **real, inverted and diminished**.
B **9. Fovea (yellow spot)** — central part of the retina; highest concentration of cones; provides sharpest vision.
B **10. Blind spot** — the point where the optic nerve leaves the eye; no photoreceptors → no vision.
B **11. Optic nerve** — carries visual signals to the brain; connects the eye to the visual cortex.
ADD Near point, far point and persistence of vision (standard facts the lecture skips) :: Least distance of distinct vision (near point) for a normal eye = **25 cm**; far point = infinity. Persistence of vision ≈ 1/16 s (basis of cinema at 24 frames/s). The brain re-inverts the inverted retinal image.

ADD Cataract & colour blindness (frequently asked, not in the lecture) :: **Cataract** — the eye lens becomes cloudy/opaque (often with age); treated by surgically replacing the lens with an artificial intra-ocular lens. **Colour blindness** — genetic (X-linked, far commoner in males) deficiency of certain cones, usually red–green; it cannot be corrected by lenses. **Night blindness** — deficiency of vitamin A (rod pigment rhodopsin).
