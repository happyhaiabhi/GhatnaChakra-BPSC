H2 12.1 Medicine & Drugs
B **Medicine/drug** — chemical substances that treat diseases or reduce pain. Treatment with compounds that destroy microorganisms without harming the body = **chemotherapy**; the compounds = **chemotherapeutic agents**.

H2 12.2 Dyes
B **Dyes** — coloured substances used in solution or dispersion on substrates (cotton, wool, silk, polyester, nylon, paper, leather, hair, fur, plastics) to give a coloured, shiny appearance.
B **Colour & theory:** colour depends on light absorption — a compound absorbing in the visible region shows the complementary colour of what it reflects (a dye absorbing blue looks yellow).
BB **Chromophores** — groups that absorb light and produce colour (nitro, azo groups etc.).
BB **Auxochromes** — groups that do not absorb light themselves (unlike chromophores) but **deepen the colour** when mixed with coloured compounds — –OH, –NH₂, –Cl, –COOH.
H3 Classification by Source
BB **Natural dyes** — from plants (nature): **alizarin, indigo**.
BB **Synthetic dyes** — man-made in the laboratory: malachite green, orange-I, orange-II, aniline yellow.
H3 Constitution
B Nitro dyes — martius yellow; **azo dyes** — aniline yellow, methyl orange, orange-I, congo red; **triphenylmethane dyes** — malachite green, magenta; **indigoid dyes** — indigo, indigosol; **anthraquinone dyes** — alizarin; **phthalein dyes** — phenolphthalein.
H3 Applications (by how they attach)
TBL Class~Mechanism / note~Examples
Acid dyes~Sodium salts of azo dyes bearing sulphonic/carboxylic acid; dye wool, silk, polyurethane; not cotton; nylon's free amino groups give high affinity~Orange-I, orange-II, congo red, methyl orange, methyl red
Basic dyes~Salts of azo/triphenylmethane dyes with amino (auxochrome) groups; attach to anionic sites of fabric; for polyesters & reinforced nylons~Aniline yellow, butter yellow, malachite green, chrysoidine G
Direct dyes~Water-soluble; applied directly from aqueous solution; need H-bonding fabrics (cotton, wool, silk, rayon, nylon)~Congo red, martius yellow
Fibre-reactive dyes~Attach by irreversible reaction with –OH/–NH₂ of fibre (cotton, wool, silk); very fast, long-lasting colour~2,4-dichloro-1,3,5-triazine derivatives
Insoluble azo dyes~Coupling of phenols/naphthols/aminophenols adsorbed on fabric (with polyurethanes, polyacrylonitrile, leather); ≈60 % of all dyes; toxic — never for food~Cosmetics, drugs, biological stains/indicators
Ingrain (developed) dyes~Insoluble in water; produced in situ on the fabric surface by coupling reactions~Para red
Vat dyes~Insoluble; first reduced to colourless soluble form (alkaline sodium hydrosulphite), applied, then re-oxidised to the coloured form (air/chromic acid); dye wool~Indigo, indigosol O
Mordant dyes~Fabric first treated with metal-ion mordant that binds dye & fibre; different metals → different colours; used for wool~Alizarin (rose-red / turkey red)

H2 12.3 Rocket Propellants
B **Rocket propellants** give the push that places rocket satellites into space. Each propellant combines two compounds: (a) an **explosive compound = fuel** and (b) an **oxidizer**.
H3 Conditions for a Good Propellant
B (1) **Ashless** — burning leaves no ash; (2) burning produces a **large volume of gases per gram** of fuel; (3) combustion proceeds at a **fast rate**.
H3 Classification (by physical state of fuel + oxidizer)
TBL Type~Fuel~Oxidizer~Notes
Solid — composite~Polymeric binder: polyurethane or polybutadiene~Ammonium perchlorate—
Solid — double base~Nitrocellulose + nitroglycerine (both in one mass)—~Once ignited they burn at a predetermined rate — **no start/stop capability**
Liquid — monopropellant~Single liquid acting as fuel & oxidizer — nitromethane, methyl nitrate—~Higher thrust than solids; thrust controllable by switching flow on/off
Liquid — bipropellant~Kerosene, alcohol, hydrazine, monomethyl hydrazine (MMH), liquid hydrogen~Liquid oxygen, nitrogen tetroxide, nitrous acid~Same advantages as monopropellants
Hybrid~Solid fuel — acrylic rubber~Liquid oxidizer — nitrous oxide—
TBLW 20,34,24,22
B **Specific impulse (I_s)** — defines the superiority/performance of a propellant: performance ∝ **flame temperature (T)** and inversely ∝ average molecular mass (M) of product gases — hotter flame & lighter exhaust gases → better propellant.

H2 12.4 Chemicals in Food
B **Food additives** — chemicals that improve quality, appearance, taste, odour & nutritive value: preservatives, flavouring agents, artificial sweeteners, dyes, antioxidants, fortifiers, emulsifiers, antifoaming agents.
B **Antioxidants** — prevent oxidation of fats in packaged food (chips, biscuits, cereals, crackers); more reactive towards oxygen; slow free-radical ageing: **BHT** (butylated hydroxytoluene), **BHA** (butylated hydroxyanisole).
B **Artificial sweeteners** — for diabetics & calorie control (sucrose/fructose add calories & cause decay):
TBL Sweetener~Key facts
Saccharin~Used as sodium/calcium salt; highly water-soluble; non-biodegradable; zero calorific value
Aspartame~Methyl ester of dipeptide (phenylalanine + aspartic acid); sugar substitute in soft drinks & cold foods
Alitame~Like aspartame but more stable
Sucralose~Trichloro derivative of sucrose; no calories, no tooth decay
Cyclamate~N-cyclohexyl sulfamate
L-Glucose~Sweet like D-sugars but no energy — our body lacks the enzymes to metabolise it
TBLW 20,80
B **Preservatives** — protect food from bacteria, yeasts, moulds: **sodium benzoate**; sodium/potassium metabisulphite (jams, squashes, pickles — SO₂ → sulphurous acid inhibits growth); salts of propionic & sorbic acid.
B **Edible colours** — improve appearance, add no nutrition.

H2 12.5 Soaps & Synthetic Detergents
H3 Soaps
B Sodium/potassium salts of higher fatty acids — lauric (C₁₁H₂₃COOH), myristic (C₁₃H₂₇COOH), palmitic (C₁₅H₃₁COOH), stearic (C₁₇H₃₅COOH), oleic (C₁₇H₃₃COOH), linoleic (C₁₇H₃₁COOH), linolenic (C₁₇H₂₉COOH).
B **Biodegradable** — sewage microorganisms oxidise them fully to CO₂ → no water pollution. **Two disadvantages:**
BB Fail in **hard water** — Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ form curdy white precipitate (scum).
BB Fail in **acidic solutions** — precipitate insoluble free fatty acids.

H3 Synthetic Detergents
B Sodium salts of long-chain alkyl hydrogen sulphates / alkylbenzene sulphonates; work even in hard water (Ca/Mg salts soluble); not completely biodegradable → water pollution.
IMG p153_0|Classification of detergents|58
TBL Type~Nature~Examples / facts
Anionic~Large anionic part~(a) Sodium alkyl sulphates — long-chain alcohols + conc. H₂SO₄ + NaOH; purely biodegradable. (b) Alkylbenzene sulphonates — Friedel-Crafts alkylation + sulphonation + NaOH; sodium dodecylbenzene sulphonate; effective in slightly acidic solutions (soaps fail there)
Cationic~Quaternary ammonium salts (Cl⁻, Br⁻, acetate); expensive; germicidal~Cetyltrimethylammonium chloride [CH₃(CH₂)₁₅N⁺(CH₃)₃]Cl⁻
Non-ionic~High-mass esters~Polyethylene glycol + stearic acid; or long-chain alcohols + excess ethylene oxide with base
TBLW 14,34,52
B **Detergent pollution:** **branched** hydrocarbon chains — bacteria cannot attack the side chains → slow degradation & accumulation in lakes/ponds/rivers.
BB **Biodegradable detergents** — straight chains (e.g. **sodium lauryl sulphate**) — microorganisms easily break them down.

H2 12.6 Fuels
B **Fuel** — any substance undergoing chemical/nuclear change to produce energy that can be converted to useful work.
B **Calorific value** — energy released per unit mass on complete combustion; **SI unit J/kg**.
IMG p154_0|Fuel classification|34
B **Renewable sources** — obtained back via biogeochemical cycles (running water → rain; solar). **Non-renewable** — fossil fuels take millions of years; use judiciously; prefer alternatives first.
H3 Properties of an Ideal Fuel
B **Ignition temperature** — easily attainable (matchstick head ignites by friction heat).
B **High calorific value.**
B **Environment-friendly** — less harmful residue; complete combustion (CNG leaves almost none).
B **Moderate, constant rate of combustion** — rapid/explosive burning is undesirable.
B **Easy availability at low cost** and **easy storage & transport**.
H3 Types of Fuels
B **By state:** solid / liquid / fuel gases (in their primary stage). **By occurrence:** natural, biofuels, fossil, artificial.
BB **Fossil fuels** — dead plants → **coal**; dead aquatic animals → **petroleum (crude oil)**; rich energy, non-renewable.
BB **Artificial fuels** — water gas & producer gas (laboratory/industrial conditions).
IMG p155_1|Calorific values of common fuels|46
TBL Solid~Liquid~Gaseous
Wood, coal, coke, cow-dung cake, paraffin wax, uranium (nuclear)~Petrol, diesel, kerosene, LPG, LNG~Natural gas (firedamp — mainly CH₄ + higher hydrocarbons + traces of H₂S); biogas; blast-furnace gas; refinery gases; oil-gasification gas
TBLW 34,28,38
B **Natural gas characteristics:** high calorific value; proper ignition temperature; easier to extract from petroleum deposits than coal; liquefiable → **LNG** transported by tankers.
B **Other gaseous fuel sources:** methane around coal beds; waste & biomass gases; industrial gases; petroleum-derived (LPG, refinery gases); fermentation (ethyl alcohol from sugarcane).
H3 Octane Number & Knocking
B **Octane number** — fuel's ability to resist **"knocking"** (premature combustion in spark-ignition/petrol engines); scaled to **iso-octane = 100**, **n-heptane = 0**; measured as **RON** (Research Octane Number).
B **Definition:** % of iso-octane in an iso-octane/n-heptane mixture matching the fuel's anti-knock quality.
B **Knocking:** premature, uneven combustion of the fuel-air mix → pressure waves → efficiency loss & engine damage; more prevalent with low-octane fuel.
B **High vs low octane:** high-compression (luxury/sports) engines need 95–100; regular petrol 87–90; premium 91–100+; racing/aviation fuels often exceed 100.
B **India:** moving toward **95 RON** and **E20** (20 % ethanol) blends for efficiency & lower emissions; indigenous 97-octane reference fuel produced; E20 mandate also raises octane.
B **Global standards:** RON & MON; pumps in many countries display **AKI** (Anti-Knock Index = average of RON & MON).
B **Octane vs cetane:** octane → petrol engines (spark-ignition); **cetane number** → diesel engines (compression-ignition), measuring ignition delay.
H3 Tetraethyl Lead (TEL)
B TEL was added to petrol as an **anti-knock agent** — suppresses premature ignition, raises octane, lets engines run at higher compression ratios & more power.
B **Phased out globally** — lead is toxic (neurotoxic, environmental hazard); remains only in some aviation gasoline (Avgas). Safer alternatives: **ethanol** and **MTBE** (methyl tertiary-butyl ether).
