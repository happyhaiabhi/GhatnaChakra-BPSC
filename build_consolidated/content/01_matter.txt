H2 1.1 What is Matter?
P **Matter** is everything in this universe that **occupies space and has mass** — it offers resistance and can be perceived directly by our senses. Air, water, stones, sand, clouds, pencils, books — everything is made up of matter (e.g. book, pen, pencil, water, air, all living beings).
B Which of these is matter — happiness, air, sandwich, thoughts, juice, eraser? → **Air, sandwich, juice and eraser**, because they have mass, occupy space and can be broken into further particles. Happiness and thoughts are not matter.

H3 Constituents of Matter
B According to the **early Indian philosophers**, every living and non-living thing is made of five basic elements called the **Panchtatva — Air, Water, Earth, Sky and Fire**. Matter is therefore a composition of these five constituents.

H2 1.2 Physical Nature of Matter — Particulate, not Continuous
B Matter is **particulate in nature** — it consists of particles (visible in the microscopic image of a cube).
B If we put a drop of red colour in water, the water turns red — the particles of the colour mix with the particles of water.
IMGS p002_1|Dilution — colour persists because millions of particles just divide themselves;; p003_0|Dissolving sugar/salt — particles slip into the spaces between water particles
B The particles of matter are **very small** and can be broken into further particles. On dilution of a colourful solution we can still see the colour — millions of particles divide themselves on dilution. We cannot see these particles with the naked eye.

H3 Characteristics of Particles of Matter
B Particles of matter have **spaces between them** — salt dissolves in water because its particles get into the spaces between water particles and a mixture is formed.
B Particles of matter are **moving all the time** — hence they possess **kinetic energy** (energy due to motion). Particles intermix on their own: salt in water, various gases in air, ink in water.
BB **Diffusion** — the process of mixing two different types of particles together. Diffusion becomes **faster on heating**, because kinetic energy of particles increases on heating.
B Particles of matter **attract each other** — the force of attraction varies in different forms of matter:
BB **Solids** have the highest force of attraction — we cannot move our hands through a solid object; the particles are so tightly bound.
BB **Gases** have the least — we can move our hands easily in air because the particles are loosely bound.
BB Liquids are intermediate — we can move our hands through water but not as freely as in air.

H2 1.3 The Three States of Matter
P Based on the force of attraction between particles, matter exists in three states — **solid, liquid and gaseous**.
H3 The Solid State
B Solids have (i) a **specific shape**, (ii) **distinct boundaries** and (iii) a **fixed volume**. e.g. sugar, iron, gold, wood.
B Less kinetic energy; particles arranged in order; force of attraction is **maximum**; very little space between particles → **cannot be compressed**.
B Rubber band, sponge and salt are all solids — a rubber band and sponge change shape only when force is applied, and each grain of salt has its own definite shape (it only appears to take the container's shape).
H3 The Liquid State
B Liquids have a **fixed volume but no fixed shape** — they take the shape of the container (also called **fluids**, as they can flow / be poured). e.g. water, milk, oil, mercury, alcohol.
B Force of attraction is less than in solids → space between particles → they can flow easily; cannot be compressed. Particles have high kinetic energy and keep moving.
B **Can other matter diffuse into liquids?** Yes — solids, liquids and gases can, because there is space between liquid particles:
BB Solid into liquid → mixing sugar in tea;  Liquid into liquid → mixing ink in water;  Gas into liquid → O₂ and CO₂ present in water.
H3 The Gaseous State
B Gases have **neither fixed volume nor fixed shape** — they completely fill the container. e.g. hydrogen, oxygen.
B Particles have the least (almost no) force of attraction, large spaces between them, **maximum kinetic energy**, move freely at high speed in any direction.
B Gases **can be compressed** easily and put into a small container, unlike solids and liquids; different gases diffuse into each other easily.

H2 1.4 Can Matter Change Its State?
P Water exists in three states — **Ice (solid), Water (liquid), Water vapour (gas)** — proof that matter can change state.
H3 Effect of Temperature (heating)
B **Solids → Liquids:** on heating, kinetic energy increases and force of attraction decreases; particles vibrate, change positions and become free.
BB **Melting point** — temperature at which a solid melts to become a liquid at atmospheric pressure (melting point of ice = 273.16 K).
BB **Fusion** — the process of melting a solid into liquid. During melting the temperature stops rising: the heat is used to diminish the force of attraction — it is "hidden".
BB **Latent heat** — the heat energy used to break the force of attraction between particles. **Latent heat of fusion** — heat energy required to change 1 kg of solid into liquid at its melting point at atmospheric pressure.
BB **Atmospheric pressure** — pressure exerted by the weight of the atmosphere.
B **Liquids → Gases:** kinetic energy rises until particles overcome all attraction and the liquid boils.
BB **Boiling point** — temperature at which a liquid starts boiling at atmospheric pressure (boiling point of water = 373 K).
BB **Latent heat of vaporisation** — heat energy required to change 1 kg of liquid into gas at its boiling point at atmospheric pressure.
IMGS p006_0|Adding heat to a solid creates a liquid (fusion);; p006_1|Boiling — latent heat of vaporisation
H3 Effect of Temperature (cooling)
B **Gases → Liquids:** kinetic energy falls; the gas turns into liquid.
BB **Condensation / Liquefaction** — converting a gas into liquid by cooling (clouds form by condensation of water vapour from Earth).
B **Liquids → Solids:** kinetic energy falls; liquid becomes solid (formation of ice).
B **Sublimation** — change of state of gas directly into solid and vice-versa. e.g. **camphor** directly evaporates into air without becoming liquid.
IMG p007_1|State-change triangle: melting, boiling, condensation, solidification & sublimation|56
H3 Effect of Pressure
B Applying pressure brings particles closer, increasing the force of attraction. On compressing and cooling, a gas changes into liquid.
B **Dry ice** — solid CO₂; directly turns into gas when pressure decreases to 1 atmosphere.

H2 1.5 Evaporation & Cooling
B **Evaporation** — change of a liquid into vapours at any temperature **below its boiling point**. Liquid particles with high kinetic energy overcome the forces of attraction without any external help.
IMG p008_0|Evaporation vs boiling|44
H3 Why does evaporation cause cooling?
B Evaporation uses the energy of liquid particles; particles absorb energy from the surroundings to compensate → surroundings cool down. e.g.
B Palms feel cool when acetone (nail-paint remover) evaporates from them.
B People sprinkle water on roofs/ground on sunny days to cool the area.
B Hot tea sips faster from a saucer than from a cup.
H3 Everyday applications
B **Cotton clothes in summer:** sweat evaporates taking energy from the body; cotton absorbs sweat and exposes it to the atmosphere → evaporation → cooling.
B **Water droplets on a cold glass:** water vapour in air condenses on contact with the cold walls of the glass (gas → liquid droplets).

H2 1.6 The Five States of Matter
P Besides solid, liquid and gas, scientists have discovered two more states: **Plasma** and **Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)**.
H3 Plasma
B Particles are **super-excited and super-energetic**, in the form of **ionised gases**.
B e.g. fluorescent tubes and neon bulbs — the gas inside gets ionised when electricity passes and the plasma glows. **The Sun and stars glow because of plasma.**
IMG p009_0|Plasma around us — Sun, stars, lightning, neon lights, plasma TV|56
H3 Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)
B Fifth state, discovered by **Albert Einstein** from the studies of Indian scientist **Satyendra Nath Bose**.
B Formed by condensing gases of extremely low densities to much lower temperatures.

H3 Combined & Ideal Gas Law
EQ P₁V₁ / T₁  =  P₂V₂ / T₂        PV = nRT
B **Combined gas law** joins Boyle's, Charles's and Gay-Lussac's laws for a fixed amount of gas.
B **Ideal gas equation** PV = nRT connects all four variables: P = pressure, V = volume, n = number of moles, R = universal gas constant, T = temperature (in Kelvin).
H3 Ideal vs Real Gases
TBL Ideal gas~Real gas
Theoretical gas that perfectly obeys the gas laws~Deviates from ideal behaviour at high pressure & low temperature
Particles assumed to have no volume~Particles have finite volume
No attractive forces between particles~Intermolecular forces exist
