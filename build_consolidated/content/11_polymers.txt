H2 11.1 Polymers & Polymerisation
B **Polymer** (Greek — "many units"): a chemical substance of **high molecular mass** formed by combining a large number of simple molecules (**monomers**).
EQ n(CH₂=CH₂) → [—CH₂—CH₂—]ₙ    ethylene → polyethylene
IMG p139_0|Polymerisation — classification mind map|72
B **Polymerisation** — the process by which monomers combine & transform into polymers: n [Monomer] → Polymer.
B **Polymers vs macromolecules:** all polymers are macromolecules, but not conversely — chlorophyll (C₅₅H₇₂O₅N₄Mg) is a macromolecule **without monomer units**, so not a polymer.

BB **Branched** — (source figure) chain branches lower packing: LDPE.

H3 Based on Molecular Forces
TBL Class~Forces~Character~Examples
Elastomers~Weakest~Rubber-like; stretchable; few cross-links let them retract~Natural rubber, buna-S, buna-N
Fibres~Strong (H-bonding)~Thread-like, woven; high tensile strength; crystalline; sharp m.p.~Nylon-66, terylene, polyacrylonitrile

Thermosetting~Cross-linked on heating~Semi-fluid low-mass resins that harden irreversibly into 3-D infusible mass~Bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde, urea-formaldehyde

H3 Based on Mode of Polymerisation
BB **Addition polymers** — monomers with double/triple bonds add up; empirical formula same as monomer. *Homopolymers* — single monomer (polythene from ethene); *copolymers* — two or more monomers (buna-S).
BB **Condensation polymers** — monomers combine with elimination of small molecules (H₂O, alcohol, HCl) — e.g. **nylon-6,6** from hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid.

H2 11.3 Types of Polymerisation
H3 Chain-Growth (Addition) Polymerisation
B Reactive intermediates — **free radical, carbocation or carbanion**; monomers are unsaturated (alkenes, alkadienes & derivatives).
BB **Free-radical** — monosubstituted alkenes; catalysts benzoyl peroxide, H₂O₂, t-butyl peroxide.
BB **Cationic** — carbocation intermediates via Lewis acids (BF₃, AlCl₃, SnCl₄) or protonic acids (H₂SO₄, HF); more stable carbocation → more reactive monomer.
BB **Anionic** — carbanion intermediate; **strong bases** as initiators.

H3 Step-Growth (Condensation) Polymerisation
B Stepwise condensation eliminating small molecules (H₂O, NH₃, HCl, ROH) — e.g. adipic acid + hexamethylenediamine; phenol + formaldehyde.

H2 11.4 Important Addition Polymers
H3 Polythene
BB **LDP (low density)** — tough, flexible, transparent, chemically inert, poor electrical conductor; moderate tensile but good tearing strength; used for insulation of electric wires, squeeze bottles, toys, flexible pipes.
BB **HDP (high density)** — close packing → high density; chemically inert, tougher & harder; containers, housewares, bottles, toys, electric insulation.

H3 Polystyrene
B Monomer styrene; thermoplastic; toys, radio & TV cabinets; rain coats, electrical insulation; hard, resistant to heat & chemicals. (Foamed → thermocol, see Unit 12 polymers list.)
H3 Polypropylene (PP)
B Propylene polymerised with **Ziegler–Natta catalyst**.
H3 Polyamides — Nylon-66 & Nylon-6
B Numbers = carbon counts of diamine & dibasic acid (6 & 6 → **nylon-66**).
BB **Nylon-66** — linear; very high tensile strength; abrasion-resistant; sheets, brush bristles, textiles.
BB **Nylon-6** — from caprolactam heated with water at high temperature.

H2 11.6 Natural & Synthetic Rubber
B **Natural rubber** — coiled linear **cis-1,4-polyisoprene**; double bonds between C2–C3 of isoprene (cis configuration) → coiled chain that stretches and recoils; only van der Waals forces (no polar groups).
B Soft & sticky; usable only 10–50 °C (softens hot, brittle cold); high water absorption; attacked by oxidising agents & solvents — limited commercial use as-is.
IMG p145_1|Natural rubber — isoprene unit|46
B **Vulcanisation** — heating rubber with **sulphur (3–5 %) + zinc oxide at ~110 °C for 20–30 min** (ZnO accelerates) introduces **–S–S– polysulphide crosslinks** between chains — limits chain motion → stronger, elastic material.
IMG p145_2|Vulcanisation cross-links|46
BB **Neoprene** — polymer of chloroprene (synthetic rubber); conveyor belts, gaskets, hoses.
BB **Buna-N** — copolymer of buta-1,3-diene + acrylonitrile; oil-resistant.

H2 11.7 Polyesters & Biodegradable Polymers
B **Polyesters** — polymers with ester linkages (e.g. dacron).
BB **PMMA (polymethylmethacrylate)** — from methylmethacrylate + organic peroxide; sold as lucite, acrylite, plexiglass, perspex; hard, transparent, resists light/heat/ageing; unbreakable lights, protective coatings, dentures, aircraft windows.
BB **Glyptal** — cross-linked polyester (ethylene glycol/glycerol + phthalic acid); thermosetting; tough non-flexible film → paints & lacquers.
BB **Terylene (Dacron)** — ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid, 420–460 K, zinc acetate + antimony trioxide catalyst; resistant to chemicals & biology; blended with wool/cotton (terycot, terywool, terysilk); magnetic tapes, conveyor belts, aprons.
IMGS p146_1|Terylene (Dacron) formation|;; p146_x1|Glyptal — polyester formation
B **Biopolymers & biodegradable polymers:** synthetic polymers are mostly non-biodegradable (polythene bags!). Biopolymers from biological systems — **polysaccharides, proteins, nucleic acids** — decompose/hydrolyse by enzymes → biodegradable. **Aliphatic polyesters** are common biodegradable polymers.
BB **PHBV** — copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid + 3-hydroxypentanoic acid.
BB **Nylon-2-nylon-6** — alternating polyamide copolymer of glycine (H₂N–CH₂–COOH) + aminocaproic acid (H₂N(CH₂)₅COOH); biodegradable.
