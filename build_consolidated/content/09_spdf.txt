H2 9.2 p-Block Elements — General Properties
IMG p091_0|General properties of p-block elements|80
IMG p091_1|General properties (continued)|80
B **Configuration:** ns² np¹⁻⁶ — the last electron enters a p-orbital (groups 13–18).
B **Variety:** metals, non-metals and metalloids together — N, O, F gaseous; Al, Sn solid with high m.p.
B **Oxidation states:** often −3 to +5+ from ns & np availability; non-metals negative, metals positive.
B **Reactivity:** F most reactive (non-metals); Ne, Ar notably inert.
B **Allotropy:** C — diamond, graphite, fullerenes; also P and S show allotropy.

H2 9.3 Group 13 — Boron Family (B, Al, Ga, In, Tl)
IMG p092_x0|Action of heat on orthoboric acid|58
B **Trends:** m.p. decreases B→Ga then rises to Tl; ionization energies 1st ≪ 2nd ≪ 3rd; metallic character increases B→Tl (**B is a non-metal**). Al — light, corrosion-resistant — used in packaging & construction.
H3 Preparation of Boron
B From boric acid route: B₂O₃(s) + 3Mg(s) → 2B(s) + 3MgO(s)
B From boron trichloride: 2BCl₃ + 3H₂(g) --1270 K--> 2B(s) + 6HCl(g);  2BCl₃(g) + 3Zn(s) --900 °C--> 2B(s) + 3ZnCl₂(s)
B Electrolysis of fused boric anhydride (B₂O₃) + MgO + MgF₂ at 1100 °C: 2MgO → 2Mg + O₂; B₂O₃ + 3Mg → 2B + 3MgO
B Thermal decomposition of boron hydrides: B₂H₆(g) --Δ--> 2B(s) + 3H₂(g)

H2 9.4 Group 14 — Carbon Family (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb)
B C — heart of organic chemistry; Si — semiconductor technology; Pb — historical pipes & paints.
B **Trends:** IE decreases C→Sn then rises to Pb; C & Si non-metals, Ge metalloid, Sn & Pb metals; **catenation** — C (and Si) form long chains.
B **Compounds of carbon:** CO burns with blue flame, turns platinum/palladium-chloride paper pink-green. CO₂ — from acids on carbonates (CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂) or burning carbon; turns lime water milky, Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O, milkiness disappears in excess CO₂ (→ Ca(HCO₃)₂); dry ice sublimes.
B **Carbides:** salt-like (C₂²⁻ acetylide / C⁴⁻ methanide — CaC₂, Al₄C₃, Be₂C); covalent (SiC — carborundum); interstitial (transition-metal lattices — WC, VC).
B **Compounds of silicon:** sodium silicate Na₂SiO₃ (fused soda ash + sand); **silicones** — Si–O–Si polymers from chlorosilane hydrolysis; **silicates** — salts of silicic acid H₄SiO₄ with sp³ tetrahedral SiO₄⁴⁻ units.

H2 9.5 Group 15 — Nitrogen Family (N, P, As, Sb, Bi)
B Atomic radii increase down the group (small rise As→Bi); oxidation states −3 to +5, +3 stability increases down; IE decreases N→Bi.
H3 Ammonia (NH₃)
B **Preparation:** NH₄Cl + NaOH → NH₃ + NaCl + H₂O;  Mg₃N₂ + 6H₂O → 3Mg(OH)₂ + 2NH₃;  **Haber's process** N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).
B **Properties:** aqueous solution basic — turns red litmus blue (NH₃ + H₂O → NH₄⁺ + OH⁻).
B **Complex formation:** Ag⁺ + NH₃ → [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺;  Cu²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺;  Cd²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cd(NH₃)₄]²⁺.
B **Precipitation of metal ions:** FeCl₃ + 3NH₄OH → Fe(OH)₃↓ (brown) + 3NH₄Cl;  AlCl₃ + 3NH₄OH → Al(OH)₃↓ (white);  CrCl₃ + 3NH₄OH → Cr(OH)₃↓ (green).

H2 9.6 Group 16 — Oxygen Family (O, S, Se, Te, Po)
IMG p096_1|Group-16 chemical properties card|56
B **Oxides:** all elements form dioxides MO₂ — SO₂ gas, SeO₂ volatile solid, TeO₂ & PoO₂ non-volatile crystalline solids. **Ozone** — unstable, decomposes to O₂; strong oxidising agent (liberates nascent oxygen).
B **Allotropes of sulphur:**
BB **Rhombic (α):** bright yellow; insoluble in water & CS₂; density 2.07 g/cm³; exists as S₈ puckered rings.
BB **Monoclinic (β):** stable above 369 K; dull yellow; soluble in CS₂; slowly changes to rhombic; also S₈ rings, different crystal symmetry.
BB **Plastic sulphur:** molten sulphur poured into cold water; amorphous; insoluble in water & CS₂.
IMG p097_0|Allotropes of sulphur & sulphuric acid properties|72
B **Sulphuric acid — properties:** powerful **dehydrating agent** (strong affinity for water); **charring** of sugar/wood/paper → black carbon mass; moderately strong oxidising agent; decomposes carbonates, bicarbonates, sulphides, sulphites, thiosulphates, nitrites at room temperature:
EQ H₂SO₄ + CaCl₂ → CaSO₄ + 2HCl↑   ·   H₂SO₄ + Na₂CO₃ → Na₂SO₄ + H₂O + CO₂↑   ·   H₂SO₄ + 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O + 2CO₂↑
B Salts like **chlorides, fluorides, nitrates, acetates, oxalates** are decomposed by hot conc. H₂SO₄, liberating their corresponding acids.

H2 9.9 d-Block Elements (Transition Metals)
IMG p102_0|d-block — four transition series|56
B **Series:** 3d — Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn; 4d — Y, Zr, Nb, Mo, Tc, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag, Cd; 5d — La, Hf, Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, Pt, Au, Hg; 6d — incomplete.
H3 Electronic Configuration
B General: **(n−1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻²**. Exceptions arise because (i) energy gap between (n−1)d and ns is small, (ii) half/full-filled sets are extra stable — seen in 3d: **Cr & Cu**; 4d: Nb, Mo, Tc, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag; 5d: Pt & Au; 6d: Rg.
H3 Atomic Radii & Density
B Across 3d series: Sc→Mn radii decrease (nucleus–valence attraction dominates); Fe/Co/Ni nearly equal; Cu→Zn increase.
B Group trend: 4d > 3d; 4d ≈ 5d (**lanthanide contraction**).
B Densities ~2–22 g/cm³; Sc < Ti < V < Cr < Mn < Fe < Co < Ni = Cu > Zn (Zn low — large atomic volume); minimum density in 3d = Sc, maximum = Ni & Cu; 3d < 4d < 5d; heaviest — **Os 22.51 and Ir 22.61 g/cm³**.
H3 Oxidation States & Coloured Ions
IMG p103_0|Oxidation states of the 3d series; chromate & dichromate ions|66
B Transition metals show **variable oxidation states** (+1 to +8 typical) as ns and (n−1)d electrons participate; zero state also occurs — Ni in Ni(CO)₄, Fe in Fe(CO)₅.
B **Colour:** partially-filled d-orbitals → d–d electronic transitions absorb visible light. **Cu²⁺ — blue; Cr³⁺ — green.**
H3 Magnetic Properties
B **Paramagnetic** — unpaired d electrons (d¹–d⁹ ions); **diamagnetic** — all paired; **ferromagnetic** — strongest paramagnetism (Fe, Co, Ni). Magnetic moment: **μ = √(n(n+2))** B.M. (n = unpaired electrons).
H3 Interstitial Compounds & Alloys
IMG p104_0|Formation of interstitial compounds — "trapped atoms"|62
B Small atoms (H, C, N) fit into lattice gaps of the d-block metal: **TiC** (extreme hardness — cutting tools), **MnN** (magnetic, electronics), **FeH** (hydrogen storage, fuel cells).
NOTE The alloy-formation block was printed twice on source p. 104 — kept once.
B **Alloys:** **brass** (Cu + Zn — malleable, acoustic); **alnico magnets** (Al + Ni + Co — strong magnets); **titanium alloys** (Ti + Al/V — aerospace, medical implants).
H3 Complexes & Catalysis
B Ligands (lone-pair donors) form **coordinate covalent bonds** with the central metal ion. **Coordination number** 2–12; geometry — octahedral, square planar, tetrahedral etc.
B Examples: [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ hexacyanoferrate(III); [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ tetraamminecopper(II); [PtCl₄]²⁻ tetrachloridoplatinate(II).
B Complexes are coloured and catalytically active; some act as enzyme cofactors or therapeutic agents.
B Transition metals are exceptional **catalysts** — they activate molecules and enable hydrogenation to CO₂ conversion; vital in petroleum refining, chemical production and renewable energy.

H2 9.10 f-Block Elements (Inner Transition Metals)
IMG p108_0|Lanthanides vs actinides — comparison table|84
IMG p108_1|Lanthanides vs actinides — properties (continued)|84
IMG p110_0|Periodic table with f-block placement|80
B Lanthanoids = **4f** series; actinoids = **5f** series; both are inner transition metals placed below the main table.
H3 Lanthanides — properties
B Silvery-white, malleable, high conductivity; high density (varies across series); high m.p./b.p. (exceptions among lighter ones); unique magnetism (para/ferromagnetic at low T); tarnish slowly (oxide layer); **+3 most common** oxidation state; similar properties throughout.
H3 Actinides — properties
B **Mostly radioactive** (α, β, γ decay); very dense (U, Pu); high m.p./b.p.; **variable valency** from 5f filling — highest +7 (unstable) for Np & Pu; highest stable **+6 for U**; magnetism at low T; allotropy.
B **Similarities:** inner transition metals; similar configurations & chemistry; many actinides radioactive (heavier lanthanides slightly so); applications — lanthanides in lighting, catalysts, magnets, electronics; actinides in nuclear reactors, research, weaponry.
