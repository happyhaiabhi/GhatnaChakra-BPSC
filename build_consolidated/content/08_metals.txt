H2 8.1 Metals
B Elements with a tendency to **lose electrons** (electropositive); position — left side and centre of the periodic table.
IMG p113_1|Metals in the periodic table — block map|74
H3 Physical Properties
B **Malleability** — can be hammered into thin sheets. **Ductility** — can be drawn into wires. **Sonorosity** — produce a singing sound when struck. **Lustre** — shiny surface.
B Good conductors of heat & electricity (good for pots, pans, wires); high density; very hard and non-brittle; high melting & boiling points.
IMGS p115_0|Lanthanides & actinides strip;; p116_0|Types of metals — commercial classification|;;
IMG p113_0|d-, f-, p-block element families chart|64
B **Exceptions (from the notes):**
BB Brittle metals — beryllium, arsenic, zinc fracture instead of deforming.
BB Alkali metals (Na, K) are soft — cut with a knife — and have low density.
BB Lead, bismuth and tungsten conduct less than other metals.
BB **Mercury** is liquid at room temperature; **gallium & caesium** melt at very low temperatures.
BB Most metals are silver-grey, but copper is reddish-brown and gold is yellow.
BB Magnetic metals: iron, cobalt, nickel.
H3 Chemical Properties
B **With oxygen:** Metal + O₂ → Metal oxide (basic)
EQ 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O   ·   2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
B With Fe the oxide depends on reactivity/heat: FeO (ferrous), Fe₂O₃ (ferric) — Fe(II) & Fe(III) oxides form under different conditions.
B **With water:** Metal + H₂O → Metal hydroxide + H₂↑
EQ 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑   ·   Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂   ·   Fe + H₂O(steam) → FeO + H₂
B **With other metal salts (displacement):** P + AB → PB + A — possible only if P is more reactive than A.
EQ Na + CuCl₂ → NaCl + Cu   ·   Cu + NaCl → no reaction   ·   Cu + Ca(NO₃)₂ → no reaction
B **Double displacement:** AB + PQ → PB + AQ;  e.g. Na₂SO₄ + CuCl₂ → NaCl + CuSO₄.
B **With non-metals:** Na + Cl₂ → NaCl;  2K + I₂ → 2KI;  Mg + H₂ → MgH₂ — metals + non-metals form **ionic compounds**.
IMG p067_0|Metals reactivity series — most to least reactive|58
H3 Types of Metals
TBL Type~Where~Character
Alkali metals (Group 1)~s-block~Highly reactive; always form +1 cations; hydroxides are water-soluble alkalis
Alkaline earth metals (Group 2)~s-block~Form +2 cations (Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻, Ca → Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻); hydroxides soluble but less than group 1
Transition metals~d-block, groups 3–12~Variable valency; colourful compounds; hard, strong, high m.p.; catalysts; Fe/Co/Ni magnetic
Inner transition metals~f-block~Lanthanides (Ce–Lu) & actinides (Th–Lr); heavy, high m.p., variable valency, many radioactive
TBLW 24,18,58
BOX Mnemonic (as in the source)::
Group-2 order "बेटा मागे कार स्कूटर बाप राजी" = Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra.
Alkali metals: most reactive metal family; soft & light; low m.p./b.p.; single valence electron (highly electropositive); give alkaline solutions with water (Na → NaOH, a strong base); used in batteries (Li-ion), pharma (Na compounds), organic synthesis (K compounds).
BOX Catalyst::
Substance that changes the rate of a reaction without participating in it. Transition metals and their compounds are widely used as catalysts.

H2 8.2 Non-metals
B Elements having a tendency to **gain or share electrons**; more electronegative than metals; 19 in number.
IMG p117_0|Non-metals at room temperature — 11 gases, 1 liquid, 7 solids|66
H3 Physical Properties
B Brittle — non-malleable & non-ductile; non-lustrous & non-sonorous; bad conductors (**exception: graphite** conducts); low density, low m.p./b.p.; generally solids & gases (**exception: bromine**, the only liquid non-metal — red & volatile).
H3 Chemical Properties
B Accept or share electrons → covalent compounds (e.g. N≡N).
IMG p117_1|Carbon shares four electrons — covalent compound formation|30
B **With oxygen:** Non-metal + O₂ → Non-metal oxide (acidic)
EQ C + O₂ → CO₂;  CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid)   ·   S + O₂ → SO₂;  SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ (sulphurous acid)
B **Electronegativity** — tendency to attract the shared pair in a covalent compound; non-metals are more electronegative than metals.

H3 Metal Alloys and Their Uses
B An **alloy** is a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals (and non-metals) — pure iron rusts easily but alloying gives desirable properties. Steel and brass are common alloys; some steels also contain non-metals.

H3 General Facts about Hydrogen
B **Most abundant element in the universe**; on Earth it exists in combined form (water, organic matter, minerals); diatomic gas (H₂).
B Shows **dual similarity** — with alkali metals (H → H⁺ + e⁻) and halogens (H + e⁻ → H⁻).

TBL Isotope~Protons~Neutrons~Electrons~Symbol

Deuterium~1~1~1~₁H²
Tritium~1~2~1~₁H³
TBLW 24,19,19,19,19
H3 Heavy Water (D₂O)
B Contains deuterium instead of protium; discovered in **1932 by H. C. Urey & E. W. Washburn**.
B **Uses:** nuclear **moderator & coolant** (slows neutrons); **tracer** in chemistry/metabolism studies; production of deuterium compounds.
H3 Hard & Soft Water
B **Soft water** lathers with soap easily; **hard water** does not — due to salts of **Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺** (bicarbonates, chlorides, sulphates).
IMG p119_0|Temporary vs permanent hardness|74
TBL Point~Temporary hard water~Permanent hard water
Cause~Bicarbonates of Ca & Mg — Ca(HCO₃)₂, Mg(HCO₃)₂~Chlorides & sulphates of Ca & Mg — CaCl₂, CaSO₄, MgCl₂, MgSO₄
Removal~Can be removed simply by boiling~Cannot be removed by simple methods; needs chemical treatment
TBLW 20,40,40
B **Effects of hard water:** soap wastage; scale (boiler) formation; problems in dyeing & laundry.
H3 Hydrogen Peroxide (H₂O₂)
B Discovered **1818 by Thenard**; pale blue liquid; m.p. −0.43 °C; b.p. 150.2 °C; density 1.47 g/cm³.
B Strong **oxidising agent** (also a reducing agent in some reactions); unstable → decomposes to water + oxygen.
B **Uses:** germicide & antiseptic; bleaching of textiles, paper, hair, leather, oils & fats; manufacture of sodium perborate & sodium carbonate; milk/food preservation; oxygen source in submarines.

H2 8.4 Sulphur & Its Important Compounds
B **Occurrence:** 0.03–0.1 % of Earth's crust; sources — gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O), Epsom salt (MgSO₄·7H₂O), barite (BaSO₄), galena (PbS), zinc blende (ZnS), copper pyrites (CuFeS₂); found in volcanic H₂S; present in proteins of egg, onion, garlic, mustard, pulses.
B Oxidised forms: H₂SO₄, H₂SO₃, peroxodisulphuric acid (H₂S₂O₈), pyrosulphuric acid (H₂S₂O₇).
TBL Compound~Facts
Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)~Colourless gas, pungent smell; liquefies at 263 K under pressure; highly water-soluble. Uses — petroleum refining; bleaching wool & silk; disinfectant & preservative; refrigerant
Sulphur hexafluoride (SF₆)~Colourless, odourless, non-toxic, octahedral; excellent electrical insulator
Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)~"King of chemicals"; colourless, oily, hygroscopic; high b.p.; very strong acid. Uses — petroleum refining; fertilisers; explosives, detergents, plastics; storage batteries; medicines (nitroglycerine)
TBLW 26,74
H3 Inert (Noble) Gases
B He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn — rare/noble gases; filled outer shells → very low reactivity; colourless, odourless, non-flammable.
TBL Gas~Uses
Helium~Balloons & airships; O₂-mix for deep-sea divers (avoids nitrogen narcosis); asthma treatment; inflating aeroplane tyres; inert atmosphere for welding reactive metals
Neon~Discharge/advertising tubes; beacon lights for air navigation (fog-piercing)
Argon~Fills electric lamps with nitrogen (more inert than N₂); fluorescent lamps
Krypton~High-intensity airport runway lamps; photographic flashes
Xenon~Powerful lamps; camera flashes; some medical anaesthesia
Radon~Cancer radiotherapy (radioactive — used carefully)
TBLW 16,84

H2 8.5 Metallurgy
B **Metallurgy** — the branch of science dealing with extraction of metals from ores and their purification.
IMG p122_0|Metallurgy steps — crushing → dressing → roasting/calcination → reduction → refining|58
H3 Basic Terms
TBL Term~Meaning
Minerals~Naturally occurring compounds of metals mixed with soil, sand, limestone, rocks
Ore~Mineral from which metal is extracted commercially at low cost & minimum effort
Gangue (matrix)~Earthy impurities (mud, silica, sand) in the ore
Flux~Substance added in smelting to promote fluidity and remove impurities as slag
Slag~Product formed when flux reacts with the gangue; e.g. CaO(flux) + SiO₂(gangue) → CaSiO₃(slag)

H3 Step 1 — Crushing & Grinding
B Ores are crushed to fine powder in **big jaw crushers & ball mills**; **pulverisation** = crushing & grinding of ore to powder.
H3 Step 2 — Dressing (Concentration) of Ores
B Removal of impurities (sand, soil, rock particles) from powdered ore. Methods:
BB **Gravity / hydrolytic separation** — needs density difference between ore and impurities.
BB **Magnetic separation** — for ores attracted by a magnet.
BB **Leaching (chemical)** — a suitable solvent dissolves the ore; insoluble impurities are filtered. e.g. bauxite + hot conc. NaOH → soluble sodium aluminate Na[Al(OH)₄]; CO₂ then precipitates Al₂O₃.

H3 Step 3 — Roasting & Calcination
IMG p122_2|Roasting vs calcination|70
TBL Point~Roasting~Calcination

TBLW 20,40,40
H3 Step 4 — Reduction of Metal Oxides
B **Highly reactive metals (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al)** — oxides cannot be reduced by carbon; extracted by **electrolytic reduction**.
B **Mildly reactive metals** — oxide + reducing agent (C, CO, H₂ or a more reactive metal):
EQ 2ZnO + C → 2Zn + CO₂   ·   Fe₂O₃ + CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
B **Least reactive metals** — oxides decompose to metal on heating alone.
