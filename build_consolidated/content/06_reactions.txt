H2 6.1 What is a Chemical Reaction?
B A chemical reaction is a **rearrangement of atoms** — one or more substances (reactants) are converted into new substances (products) with different properties. The five primary types are combination, decomposition, single displacement, double displacement and combustion/redox.

H3 The five primary types
TBL Type~General form~Example
Combination (synthesis)~A + B → AB~C + O₂ → CO₂;  2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Decomposition~AB → A + B~CaCO₃ --heat--> CaO + CO₂
Single displacement~A + BC → AC + B~Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
Double displacement~AB + CD → AD + CB~AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
Redox (oxidation–reduction)~electron transfer~one substance oxidised (loses e⁻), other reduced (gains e⁻)
TBLW 28,26,46
B **Combustion** — a substance (usually a hydrocarbon) reacts with oxygen releasing heat & light: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.
H3 Other important classifications
B **Neutralisation** — double displacement of acid + base → salt + water: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O.
B **Precipitation** — a double displacement producing an insoluble solid (precipitate).
B **Endothermic vs Exothermic** — endothermic absorbs energy; exothermic releases it (see 6.4).

H2 6.3 Effects of Oxidation in Everyday Life
B **Corrosion** — a metal is attacked by substances around it (moisture, acids etc.):
BB Iron articles get a reddish-brown coating — **rusting of iron**.
BB Black coating on **silver** and green coating on **copper** — other examples of corrosion.
B **Rancidity** — fats and oils get oxidised; smell and taste change. Antioxidants are added to fatty/oily foods; air-tight containers slow oxidation; chip bags are flushed with **nitrogen** to prevent oxidation.

H2 6.4 Exothermic vs Endothermic Reactions
IMG p071_0|Energy profiles — exothermic vs endothermic|78
TBL Point~Exothermic~Endothermic
Energy change~Releases energy (heat, light, sound) to surroundings~Absorbs energy from surroundings
Temperature~Surroundings become hot~Surroundings become cold
Enthalpy~Negative (ΔH < 0)~Positive (ΔH > 0)
Bonding~Forms stronger bonds; releases excess energy~Absorbs energy to break bonds
Examples~Combustion of fuels, rusting of iron, nuclear fission, mixing acids with bases, freezing water~Photosynthesis, melting ice, evaporation, thermal decomposition, dissolving ammonium nitrate in water
TBLW 18,41,41
BOX Exam pointers::
Exothermic feels hot, endothermic feels cold.
Respiration = redox + decomposition; baking with baking soda = decomposition + displacement; vinegar + baking soda = double displacement + gas evolution; burning candle = combustion.
Rusting = redox reaction; cooking = decomposition.
