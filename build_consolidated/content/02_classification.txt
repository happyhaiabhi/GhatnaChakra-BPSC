H2 2.2 Pure Substances
B A material containing **only one type of particle** is a pure substance. Pure substances are further divided into **elements** and **compounds**.

H3 Element
B A pure substance that **cannot be broken down** into simpler substances by chemical means; made of atoms with the same number of protons.
B Elements are split into three classes on the basis of physical & chemical properties: **(1) Metals (2) Non-metals (3) Metalloids** — position, properties and the full comparison table are given in **Unit 8**.
BOX Remember::
Most of the elements in the periodic table are metals. There are six metalloids — boron, silicon, germanium, arsenic, antimony, tellurium. Of the 17 non-metals, 12 are gases; all others are solids except **bromine**, a red volatile liquid.

H3 Compound
B A substance composed of **two or more different elements chemically bonded in a fixed ratio**; formed by chemical reactions.

H3 Organic vs Inorganic Compounds
TBL Point~Organic compounds~Inorganic compounds
Composition~Always contain C and H, often O, N, S or P~Typically lack C–H bonds
Bonding~Covalent bonds~Often ionic bonds
Properties~Gases, liquids or low-melting solids; generally insoluble in water~High-melting solids, often water-soluble
Source~Mostly from living organisms~From non-living sources like minerals
Examples~Glucose, methane, proteins, sugar, DNA~Salt, metals, water, CO₂
TBLW 16,42,42
B Organic: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, plastics.  Inorganic: NaCl, H₂O, HCl.
B **Exceptions** — some carbon substances behave like mineral compounds and are classified as inorganic: CO₂, CO, carbonates (CaCO₃), cyanides.

H3 Ionic vs Covalent Compounds
TBL Point~Ionic compounds~Covalent compounds
Bonding mechanism~Electron **transfer** (electrostatic attraction)~Electron **sharing** (directional shared pairs)
Elements~Usually metal + non-metal~Two or more non-metals
State & m.p./b.p.~Hard, brittle crystalline solids; high m.p./b.p.~Gases, liquids or soft solids; low m.p./b.p.
Conductivity~Conduct when molten or dissolved (not as solids)~Generally poor conductors
Solubility~Often soluble in water~Tend to be insoluble (polar covalent can be exceptions)
Structure~Crystal lattices~Discrete molecules or network solids
Examples~NaCl, CaO, (NH₄)₃P~H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, SF₆
TBLW 20,40,40

H3 Physical & Chemical Properties of Compounds
B **Physical properties** — measured without changing chemical identity: melting/boiling points (defined, distinct), density, solubility, electrical conductivity (important for ionic compounds in liquid/aqueous state), appearance (colour, odour, state).
B **Chemical properties** — ability to transform into new substances: reactivity (with oxygen/acids), flammability, toxicity, heat of combustion, corrosion resistance.

H2 2.3 Mixtures
B A mixture has **two or more substances in any ratio**; primarily of two types — **homogeneous** and **heterogeneous**.
TBL Point~Homogeneous mixture~Heterogeneous mixture
Composition~Uniform throughout (single phase); components indistinguishable~Non-uniform; two or more distinct phases
Visibility~Components not seen by naked eye~Components easily seen
Separation~Difficult — needs distillation etc.~Easy — filtration etc.
Also called~Solutions~Suspensions or colloids
Examples~Sugar solution, air, salt water, saline, vodka, brass~Oil & water, salt + sugar, suspensions, salad, pizza, soil, trail mix
TBLW 16,42,42
B **Phase** — any distinct portion of matter uniform throughout in composition and properties.

H2 2.4 Colloids
B **Colloids** are heterogeneous mixtures where microscopic insoluble particles (**1 nm – 1000 nm**, i.e. 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁴ cm) of one substance (**dispersed phase**) are scattered throughout another (**dispersion medium**). They appear homogeneous but are not.
B **Key characteristics:**
BB **Stability** — particles do not settle on standing.
BB **Filterability** — pass through ordinary filter paper but separated by semipermeable membranes / centrifuges.
BB **Tyndall effect** — scatter light, making a light beam visible (fog, milk).
BB **Brownian movement** — continuous zig-zag motion due to bombardment by dispersion-medium molecules.

H3 Types of Colloids
TBL Colloid~Dispersed phase in medium~Examples
Sol~Solid in liquid~Paint, cell fluids, blood
Solid sol~Solid in solid~Coloured gemstones, alloys
Emulsion~Liquid in liquid~Milk, hair cream, mayonnaise
Foam~Gas in liquid~Whipped cream, soap lather
Solid foam~Gas in solid~Pumice stone, foam rubber
Aerosol~Liquid in gas~Fog, mist, hairspray
Solid aerosol~Solid in gas~Smoke, dust
Gel~Liquid in solid~Jelly, butter, cheese
TBLW 20,40,40
B **Components:** dispersed phase = "solute-like" particles (smaller amount); dispersion medium = "solvent-like" medium (larger amount).
B **Applications:** medicines are colloidal for better absorption (argyrol); water treatment (alum coagulation); paint, ink, rubber and food industries; blood is a colloid — specialized colloidal solutions used in medical treatments.

H2 2.5 Separation of Mixtures
P Methods use physical properties — size, solubility, boiling point, magnetism — to isolate components **without changing their chemical identity**.
TBL Method~What it separates~Example
Filtration~Insoluble solid from liquid~Sand from water
Evaporation~Soluble solid from liquid~Salt from water
Distillation~Liquids with different boiling points~Alcohol & water
Fractional distillation~Liquids with closer boiling points~Crude-oil components
Magnetic separation~Magnetic from non-magnetic material~Iron filings
Chromatography~Different rates of movement across a stationary phase~Separating dyes
Centrifugation~Particles by density at high spin~Blood components
Sublimation~Sublimable solid from non-sublimable~Iodine / ammonium chloride
Crystallisation~Purification by hot-solvent recrystallisation~Purifying solids
TBLW 22,46,32
