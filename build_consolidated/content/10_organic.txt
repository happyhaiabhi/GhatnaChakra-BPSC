H2 10.3 Hydrocarbons
B Compounds containing only **carbon & hydrogen** — CH₄ (methane), C₂H₄ (ethene), C₂H₂ (ethyne).
IMG p128_1|Classification of hydrocarbons — flowchart|58
H3 Saturated Hydrocarbons (Alkanes, CₙH₂ₙ₊₂)
B Only **single covalent bonds**; also called **paraffins** (less reactive); first member — **methane**.
IMG p129_0|Alkanes — examples|40
B **Homologous series:** compounds with similar physical & chemical properties, successive members differing by a **–CH₂–** unit (alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, alcohols, aldehydes…).
B **Properties of alkanes:** non-polar; weak van der Waals forces; first four members gases, C₅–C₁₇ liquids, C₁₈ onwards solids; low reactivity.
H3 Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ)
B At least one **double bond**; e.g. ethene, propene, butene, pentene — first member **ethene (ethylene)**.
IMG p129_1|Alkenes — examples|40
B **Properties:** non-polar; first 3 members gases, C₅–C₁₈ liquids, C₁₉+ gases (source wording); colourless & odourless (exception ethene); insoluble in water.
H3 Alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂)
B At least one **triple bond**; e.g. ethyne, propyne, butyne, pentyne — first member **ethyne (acetylene)**.
IMG p129_2|Alkynes — examples|40
B **Properties:** non-polar; first 3 gases, C₅–C₁₃ liquids, C₁₄+ solids; odourless & colourless (exception ethyne); insoluble in water.

B Ring of carbon atoms (≥ 3) instead of a chain; divided into **alicyclic** and **aromatic** compounds.
H3 Alicyclic Hydrocarbons (non-aromatic)
B Cyclic hydrocarbons with single/double/triple bonds arranged in any (non-alternate) fashion; e.g. cyclopropane, cyclobutane.

BB **Homocyclic / carbocyclic** — ring of only carbon atoms → cycloalkanes (single bonds), cycloalkenes (double), cycloalkynes (triple).
BB **Heterocyclic** — ring contains atoms other than carbon.

B Cyclic with **alternating single & double bonds**; most have **pleasant smell**; divided into **benzenoids** (contain benzene ring), **non-benzenoids** (no benzene ring) and **heterocyclic aromatics** (other atoms in ring).

H2 10.8 Methane, Ethene, Ethyne, Ethanol & Ethanoic Acid
H3 Methane (CH₄) — 1st alkane
B Also called **marsh gas**; ≈ 80–85 % of natural gas; colourless, odourless; produced in paddy fields.
B **Preparation:** Al₄C₃ + H₂O → Al(OH)₃ + CH₄↑;  CH₃COONa + NaOH --Δ--> Na₂CO₃ + CH₄ (sodium ethanoate route).
IMG p127_1|Methane — Lewis structure|30
H3 Ethene (C₂H₄) — 1st alkene
B Plant hormone — responsible for **ripening of fruits**; colourless with sweet smell.
B **Preparation:** ethanol --conc. H₂SO₄, Δ--> ethene + H₂O — conc. H₂SO₄ acts as a **dehydrating agent**.
IMG p136_x1|Ethene preparation from ethanol|46
H3 Ethyne (C₂H₂) — 1st alkyne
B Structure H–C≡C–H; colourless gas with a smell; lighter than air.
B **Preparation:** CaC₂ + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + C₂H₂↑.
H3 Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)
B Liquid at room temperature; common organic solvent; consumption causes drunkenness — 100 % (absolute) ethanol is lethal.
B **Reactions:**
BB With sodium: 2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa (sodium ethoxide) + H₂.
BB Neutral (pH ≈ 7).
BB Dehydration with hot conc. H₂SO₄ gives unsaturated ethene.
IMGS p136_2|Ethanol — Lewis structure|;; p137_0|Ethanol + sodium → sodium ethoxide + H₂
H3 Ethanoic Acid (CH₃COOH) — carboxylic acid
B Called **glacial acetic acid** — low melting point (~15–17 °C); 5–8 % solution = **vinegar**.
B **Esterification** — alcohol + carboxylic acid --H₂SO₄--> **ester** (sweet-smelling) + water:
EQ CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
B **Saponification** — opposite process: ester + base → alcohol + metallic salt of carboxylic acid (**soap formation reaction**).
IMG p137_1|Saponification reaction|58
B **With bases:** CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa (sodium ethanoate) + H₂O.
B **With carbonates:** 2CH₃COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COOK (potassium ethanoate) + H₂O + CO₂.
